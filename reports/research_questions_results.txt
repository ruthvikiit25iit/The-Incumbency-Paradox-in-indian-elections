QUESTION 1: Do incumbents have a higher observed winning rate?

Purpose
Compare the observed candidate win rates for incumbents and non-incumbents.

Data and scope
- Input: signed_margin_analysis/data_files/elections_with_margin_history_and_party_vote_share_from_field.csv
- Election rows: poll_type == 0
- Winner definition: position == 1
- Rows with missing incumbent status excluded: 1,085
- No deposit-lost candidates are present in this input file; the rates apply to the candidates retained in this file.

Stage 1: Observed winning rates
- Incumbents: 2,837 wins / 4,900 candidates = 57.90%
- Non-incumbents: 5,171 wins / 12,855 candidates = 40.23%
- Difference (incumbent minus non-incumbent): 17.67 percentage points

Stage 2: 95% confidence intervals
- Incumbent win rate, Wilson interval: 56.51% to 59.27%
- Non-incumbent win rate, Wilson interval: 39.38% to 41.08%
- Difference, Newcombe interval: 16.04 to 19.29 percentage points
- SciPy provides the normal critical value used in the interval calculations.

Interpretation
The difference interval does not include zero, indicating different observed win rates in this dataset. This is an association, not proof that incumbency caused the difference.

Limitations
The basic intervals treat candidate entries as independent. Candidates who appear in multiple elections may make that assumption imperfect.
